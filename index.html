02 / Abstract

Abstract

We propose an improving framework for Video Super-Resolution and Enhancement in Low-Light Environment (VSRELL++) that recovers normal light high-resolution sequences from low-light low-resolution inputs. Due to the complex coupling of multiple degradations, this joint task has received relatively little attention. Our method jointly models illumination enhancement and spatial-temporal super-resolution to disentangle intertwined degradations.

Specifically, we introduce an illumination-noise co-optimization module that employs dynamic window partitioning to explicitly model physical priors of illumination variations and noise distributions, effectively suppressing cross-frame noise accumulation and illumination flickering. Moreover, an illumination-sensitive feature propagation mechanism utilizes hierarchical illumination-sensing gating to adaptively modulate feature propagation, enhancing high-quality features while suppressing error accumulation in low-light regions.

Finally, a self-distillation consistency training strategy enforces brightness continuity through multi-level illumination augmentation and student--teacher distillation, ensuring temporal consistency across video sequences. Extensive experiments demonstrate that VSRELL++ significantly outperforms state-of-the-art methods in both quantitative metrics and visual quality.
